Investigate whether scaling Supervised Fine-Tuning from 100 trajectories (`v2`) to 500 trajectories (`v3`) improves model performance, fixes premature multi-step termination, and surpasses both the 70.0% SFT+Prompt baseline and the 80.0% zero-shot base model baseline.

## 5. Failure Taxonomy Breakdown for SFT-500

Of the 21 failed tasks out of 60 total benchmark tasks:
1. **A. Unnecessary Tool Over-Activation (8 tasks / 38.1% of failures):** The model invoked tools on 83.33% of `no_tool` direct knowledge queries.
2. **F. Premature Termination (7 tasks / 33.3% of failures):** The model stopped early on multi-step chains (e.g. outputting final answer after step 1 or 2 instead of executing required steps 3 and 4).
3. **G. Incorrect Final Answer (4 tasks / 19.0% of failures):** Arithmetic calculation or exact lookup formatting errors.
4. **H. Exact Match Formatting Artifacts (2 tasks / 9.5% of failures):** Extra text wrapping in string manipulation tasks.

---

## 6. Key Insights & Decision Rule Findings

1. **More Data Did NOT Equal Better Accuracy:** Scaling from 100 to 500 trajectories without adjusting the tool-bias ratio degraded overall accuracy from **70.0% down to 65.0%** (and below the **80.0%** base model baseline).
2. **Tool Over-Activation Root Cause:** SFT v3 contained a 65% tool-required dataset bias (only 35% `no_tool` trajectories). This caused the fine-tuned model to develop strong bias toward invoking tools even when direct parametric answers were requested.
3. **Repetition Fully Resolved:** Zero infinite loops occurred (0 repetition failures).
